What is multi-service connectivity?

Several services.
One planned handoff.

Multi-service connectivity delivers Internet, private cloud and private network services over a common access circuit, with logical separation between services. Axclusive can present them on one port or use customer premises equipment (CPE) to provide separate physical handoff ports.

For your team, the value is fewer separate access arrangements to coordinate and a design that accounts for the services together. Capacity, routing, security and resilience still need to be specified for each service.

Start with the customer’s working day

One business. More than one destination.

Your staff use the Internet. Your applications reach cloud workloads. Your servers exchange data with another site. Plan those connections around the job each one needs to do.

Scenario 01

An office growing into hybrid cloud

Your team needs Internet access while internal applications depend on workloads in AWS or Azure. Combine DIA, private cloud access and an office-to-data-centre connection in one access design.

Plan around: Discuss cloud regions, application flows and the capacity each service needs.

Scenario 02

A colocation rack with several roles

Your rack serves public users, connects to private systems and exchanges data with a cloud environment. Plan IP transit, private Ethernet and cloud connectivity together.

Plan around: Use separate VLAN handoffs or CPE breakout to match the router, firewall and other equipment.

Scenario 03

A migration with old and new systems

During a data centre or cloud migration, your current systems and new environment may need to operate together. Plan the transfer path alongside normal Internet traffic.

Plan around: Agree peak transfer requirements, change windows and the steps to remove temporary connections.

Scenario 04

Backups that must finish before morning

Production traffic continues while backups or replication move to a second site or cloud destination. Size and separate the services around both workloads.

Plan around: Define the backup window, simultaneous traffic, recovery needs and any configured service policies.

Scenario 05

Different equipment, different handoffs

The firewall, WAN router and other devices may need separate physical connections. CPE breakout can present services on multiple ports from the same access circuit.

Plan around: Confirm port types, VLAN mapping, equipment ownership and the support boundary.

Scenario 06

A platform adding another destination

An expanding application may need another cloud or private endpoint. Review whether the existing access capacity and handoff can support the addition.

Plan around: Check feasibility, available capacity and provisioning requirements before ordering a new circuit.

Fit the handoff to your equipment

One port—or separate ports through CPE.

The services can stay logically separate while sharing physical access. Choose how your equipment receives them.

Single-port handoff

Use a VLAN-aware device to receive the agreed services on one physical port. This can suit teams that manage their own service separation and routing.

Discuss: VLAN tags, interface type, service bandwidths, routing responsibilities and the aggregate port limit.

CPE breakout

Use customer premises equipment to present the services on multiple physical ports. Connect different devices to the handoffs they require.

Discuss: Number and type of ports, VLAN mapping, CPE capacity, management and equipment support.

Multiple CPE ports still share the upstream access circuit. They are not independent physical paths.

Simplify delivery. Design resilience deliberately.

One circuit should not be mistaken for two paths.

Consolidating services can reduce separate access and handoff requirements. It also means those services can share an access-circuit failure.

For critical workloads, discuss separate access paths, equipment, power, cloud connections and failover. Confirm which risks are shared and what the proposed design protects against.

A resilient cloud interconnect does not, by itself, make the customer’s last-mile connection resilient. Test the complete path against the agreed requirements.

Cloud & multi-service connectivity FAQ

Is Cloud Connect the same as multi-service connectivity?+
No. Cloud Connect describes connectivity to a cloud environment. Multi-service connectivity describes delivering several services through common physical access. A private cloud connection can be one of those services alongside Internet and private Ethernet.
Which clouds can I connect to?+
Discuss AWS Direct Connect, Microsoft Azure ExpressRoute and Google Cloud Interconnect requirements with Axclusive. Specify the cloud regions, accounts, workloads and intended connection type so availability and configuration can be confirmed.
Can Internet, cloud and private Ethernet share one port?+
Yes. Axclusive can combine services on one port or circuit with logical separation. CPE breakout can provide separate physical service handoffs. The agreed capacity and service configuration determine what can be delivered.
What speeds are available?+
Axclusive offers 1G–100G access options for this solution. Aggregate service demand must fit the agreed physical capacity and service policies. A 10G port does not provide 10G simultaneously to every service on it.
Does a private cloud connection include encryption?+
Not automatically. Private routing and encryption are different controls. Confirm encryption requirements, supported methods and responsibility for configuration as part of the design.
Can I add a service to an existing circuit?+
Ask the team to check spare capacity, interface capability, service availability and configuration. An additional service may be possible without new physical access, but this must be confirmed for the existing circuit.
Do I keep control of my routing?+
Customer-controlled routing is supported by the existing service model. Agree the handoff, VLANs, routing boundaries and security responsibilities. Additional management can be discussed as a separate scope.
Is one shared circuit suitable for critical workloads?+
It depends on the resilience requirements. Multiple logical services can share the same physical failure point. Discuss diverse access and end-to-end failover rather than relying on service separation alone.
